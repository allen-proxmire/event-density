## 7. Constructive Necessity

The derivation has five steps.

### 7.1 The adjacency band as the substrate-level carrier of kinematic content

Primitive P04 §1.5 supplies the four-band partition of substrate bandwidth: $b_K = b_K^\mathrm{int} + b_K^\mathrm{adj} + b_K^\mathrm{env} + b_K^\mathrm{com}$, with the four bands carrying mutually orthogonal structural content (Paper #11 §7.1 establishes this orthogonality).

Of the four bands, the **adjacency band** $b^\mathrm{adj}$ is the unique carrier of kinematic content — coupling between a chain and its immediate spatial neighborhood. The internal band sustains the chain's rule-content (mass, statistics), the environmental band couples to the broader bath (decoherence-relevant), and the commitment-reserve band depletes during commitment events. None is kinematic; the adjacency band is.

The substrate-level "kinetic energy" — the cost of changing position — is therefore a property of $b^\mathrm{adj}$. The structural question of this paper is: what kinetic operator emerges from the adjacency-bandwidth structure?

### 7.2 The Fourier-conjugate partition

Paper #11 §7.2 establishes the partition $b^\mathrm{adj} = b_x + b_p$ with $b_x$ encoding spatial proximity and $b_p$ encoding phase-coherent propagation. Paper #12 establishes that $b_p$ is Fourier-conjugate to $b_x$ via Stone's theorem on spatial translations, with the momentum operator $\hat{p} = -i\hbar\nabla$ as the unique self-adjoint generator.

The kinetic energy of a chain is the substrate-level cost of bandwidth flow between adjacent positions — i.e., the cost of having $b_p$ content (phase-coherent propagation) at the chain's locus. Without $b_p$ content, the chain is purely $b_x$-localized and has no kinetic content. With $b_p$ content, the chain propagates phase-coherently across spatial regions, which carries a kinetic-energy cost.

The kinetic operator $\hat{T}$ must therefore be a *function of $\hat{p}$* — the operator that diagonalizes the momentum-adjacency content $b_p$.

### 7.3 Quadratic structure from substrate constraints

Three substrate-level constraints force $\hat{T}$ to be quadratic in $\hat{p}$:

**(i) Rotational isotropy (C4).** The substrate has no preferred spatial direction. $\hat{T}$ must be a scalar function of $\hat{p}$:
$$
\hat{T} = f(|\hat{p}|^2),
$$
since the only rotationally-invariant scalar built from $\hat{p}$ is $|\hat{p}|^2 = \hat{p}\cdot\hat{p}$.

**(ii) Bandwidth additivity (C2).** The substrate-level bandwidth content $b_p$ is additive over disjoint channel decompositions. The kinetic energy associated with each momentum-component must be additive: for two disjoint momentum-content contributions $b_{p_1}$ and $b_{p_2}$ with $b_p = b_{p_1} + b_{p_2}$, the total kinetic energy is the sum of contributions. Bandwidth additivity at the substrate level propagates to kinetic-energy additivity: $T(p_1 + p_2) = T(p_1) + T(p_2)$ for orthogonal contributions.

For a quadratic $T = cp^2$, orthogonal addition gives $T(p_1 + p_2) = c|p_1 + p_2|^2 = cp_1^2 + cp_2^2 + 2c\,p_1\cdot p_2 = T(p_1) + T(p_2)$ when $p_1 \cdot p_2 = 0$ (orthogonal). The Pythagorean-style additivity holds.

For linear $T = c|p|$, orthogonal addition gives $T(p_1 + p_2) = c\sqrt{p_1^2 + p_2^2} \neq T(p_1) + T(p_2)$ — fails additivity.

For higher-order $T = c|p|^{2n}$ with $n > 1$, orthogonal addition similarly fails the Pythagorean structure.

The quadratic form is the unique exponent satisfying both rotational isotropy and orthogonal-additivity simultaneously.

**(iii) Non-relativistic-limit scope (C5).** Higher-power terms $|\hat{p}|^{2n}$ for $n \geq 2$ are suppressed by powers of $(v/c)^{2(n-1)}$ in the non-relativistic limit. In the strict $c \to \infty$ limit, only the $|\hat{p}|^2$ term survives. C5 forbids higher-order corrections at the substrate level (they appear as post-Newtonian corrections in the relativistic regime, Paper #7 territory).

Combining (i), (ii), (iii):
$$
\hat{T}(\hat{p}) = a_1\,|\hat{p}|^2
$$
for some coefficient $a_1$ to be determined.

### 7.4 Mass scaling from Galilean covariance

The coefficient $a_1$ is fixed by the Galilean Lie algebra acting on the bandwidth-flow structure.

In Paper #6 §7.4, the Galilean commutator $[\hat{H}, \hat{K}_i] = -i\hbar\hat{p}_i$ with boost generator $\hat{K}_i = m\hat{x}_i - t\hat{p}_i$ produced $a_1 = 1/(2m)$ as the integration Jacobian. The same argument applies here, but motivated by the substrate-level requirement that bandwidth flow $b_p$ at locus $u$ must be Galilean-covariant — i.e., the form of the kinetic operator must be invariant under Galilean boosts of the chain's reference frame.

A Galilean boost changes the chain's momentum: $\hat{p} \to \hat{p} + m\hat{v}_\mathrm{boost}$. The kinetic operator must transform such that the substrate-level bandwidth-flow content remains structurally the same. Solving the commutator $[\hat{H}, \hat{K}_i] = -i\hbar\hat{p}_i$ on the bandwidth-flow structure (analogous to Paper #6 §7.4 but applied to $b_p$-mediated flow rather than directly on the Hilbert-space generator):
$$
\partial_{\hat{p}_i}\hat{T} = \frac{\hat{p}_i}{m} \implies \hat{T} = \frac{|\hat{p}|^2}{2m}.
$$

The factor of $1/(2m)$ is the integration Jacobian — same factor as Paper #6, arrived at via a different premise (adjacency-bandwidth flow vs. direct Galilean closure on the Hilbert-space Hamiltonian).

The two derivations converge: bandwidth-flow Galilean covariance + Hilbert-space Galilean closure produce the same kinetic operator with the same mass-scaling coefficient.

### 7.5 Composite result: $\hat{T} = -\hbar^2\nabla^2/(2m)$

Combining §7.3 (quadratic form) and §7.4 (mass scaling):
$$
\hat{T} = \frac{|\hat{p}|^2}{2m}.
$$
Substituting $\hat{p} = -i\hbar\nabla$ from Paper #12:
$$
\hat{T} = \frac{(-i\hbar\nabla)^2}{2m} = -\frac{\hbar^2\nabla^2}{2m}.
$$

This is the **Laplacian kinetic operator** of standard non-relativistic quantum mechanics. The form is FORCED by adjacency-bandwidth partition + rotational isotropy + bandwidth additivity + non-relativistic scope + Galilean covariance. The numerical value of $m$ is INHERITED.

**Equivalence with Paper #6 route.** Paper #6 §7.3-§7.4 produces the same operator via Galilean Lie algebra closure $[\hat{H}, \hat{K}_i] = -i\hbar\hat{p}_i$ on the Hilbert-space Hamiltonian. The present paper produces it via the same Galilean commutator applied to the adjacency-bandwidth-flow structure. Both routes use the Galilean Lie algebra; both arrive at the same integration Jacobian. The difference is what the commutator *acts on*: Paper #6 applies it directly to $\hat{H}$ on the Hilbert space; the present paper applies it to the substrate-level $b_p$-mediated flow structure. The two converge.

## 8. Exclusion Arguments

### 8.1 A1 — Linear kinetic term

Vector $\alpha \cdot \hat{p}$ violates rotational isotropy (C4): the constant vector $\alpha$ would break rotational symmetry of the participation manifold. Scalar $|\hat{p}|c$ violates orthogonal-additivity (§7.3-(ii)): $|p_1 + p_2| \neq |p_1| + |p_2|$ for orthogonal contributions. Furthermore, $|\hat{p}|c$ is the massless-particle dispersion, contradicting the massive non-relativistic scope (C5).

### 8.2 A2 — Higher-order spatial derivatives

Higher-order terms $|\hat{p}|^{2n}$ ($n \geq 2$) violate the non-relativistic limit (C5): they are suppressed by $(v/c)^{2(n-1)}$ and vanish in $c \to \infty$. They also fail the Pythagorean-additivity argument of §7.3-(ii): $(p_1^2 + p_2^2)^n \neq p_1^{2n} + p_2^{2n}$ for $n \neq 1$.

### 8.3 A3 — Non-local kinetic operators

A non-local kernel $\hat{T}\psi(x) = \int K(x, y)\psi(y)\,dy$ violates the locality content of the substrate primitives (C4 spatial homogeneity is a *local* invariance fact). Furthermore, the Fourier-conjugate structure of $(b_x, b_p)$ (Paper #11) supplies a *local* kinetic operator in the position representation: the Laplacian is a differential operator (local), not an integral operator (non-local).

### 8.4 A4 — Real-valued kinetic generators

Real-valued $\hat{T}$ would produce non-unitary time evolution: $e^{-i\hat{T}t/\hbar}$ with real $\hat{T}$ is unitary (the $i\hbar$ on the LHS makes the exponent imaginary). What A4 actually proposes is a *real* coefficient on $\partial_t$ — but unitarity (C7 inherited from Paper #4) forces the imaginary coefficient $i\hbar$.

The kinetic operator itself ($\hat{T} = -\hbar^2\nabla^2/(2m)$) is real-valued in the position representation, with $\hat{p}^2 = (-i\hbar\nabla)^2 = -\hbar^2\nabla^2$ producing a real operator. This is *consistent* with Hermiticity (the kinetic operator's spectrum is real and non-negative).

### 8.5 A5 — Non-Galilean kinetic structure

Lorentz-covariant kinetic structure ($\sqrt{|\hat{p}|^2 c^2 + m^2c^4}$, $i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu$) operates outside C5's non-relativistic scope. These are appropriate kinetic operators in the relativistic regime (Paper #7's Dirac equation territory), but their non-relativistic limit reduces to $\hat{p}^2/(2m) + \mathcal{O}((v/c)^2)$, recovering the substrate-forced form.

### 8.6 A6 — Anisotropic kinetic energy

Direction-dependent kinetic operators $\hat{T} = a_x\hat{p}_x^2 + a_y\hat{p}_y^2 + a_z\hat{p}_z^2$ with $a_x \neq a_y \neq a_z$ violate rotational isotropy (C4). The substrate has no preferred direction; the kinetic operator must be a scalar function of $|\hat{p}|^2$.

Anisotropic kinetic operators do appear in condensed-matter contexts (e.g., effective-mass tensors in semiconductors), but they arise from lattice symmetry-breaking at higher levels of structure, not at the substrate level. The substrate-forced form is isotropic.

### 8.7 A7 — Position-momentum cross terms in $\hat{T}$

Cross terms $\hat{x}\hat{p}$ or $\hat{p}\hat{x}$ in the *free* kinetic operator violate translation invariance: $\hat{x}$-dependence would break the spatial-homogeneity content (C4). Paper #6 §7.2 (kinetic-plus-potential decomposition) shows that translation invariance forbids $\hat{x}$ in the kinetic part. Cross terms appear in gauge-coupled Hamiltonians via $(\hat{p} - eA(\hat{x})/c)^2$, but the $A$ field is downstream of Paper #5 (gauge sector) and outside the gauge-coupling-free scope of the present paper.

### 8.8 A8 — Wrong mass-scaling coefficient

A coefficient $c \neq 1/2$ in $\hat{T} = c|\hat{p}|^2/m$ would correspond to a different Galilean commutator structure. The Galilean Lie algebra fixes the coefficient as the integration Jacobian of $\partial_{\hat{p}_i}\hat{T} = \hat{p}_i/m$, which integrates uniquely to $|\hat{p}|^2/(2m)$ (the factor of $1/2$ from $\int p\,dp = p^2/2$). Any other coefficient would violate the Galilean Lie algebra (C5 inherited via Galilean Lie algebra structure).

Adopting $\hat{T} = \hat{p}^2/(2m)$ as a foundational axiom is downstream of the substrate forcing. The present paper produces the operator from substrate primitives; treating it as a postulate is a presentation choice.

## Appendix A — Derivation Chain and Glossary

### A.1 The substrate-level Pythagorean additivity argument

The substrate-level bandwidth additivity (C2) extends to the momentum-adjacency partition $b_p$. For two orthogonal momentum contributions $p_1 \perp p_2$ with $p_1 \cdot p_2 = 0$, the total kinetic-energy contribution is the sum:
$$
T(|p|^2) = T(|p_1 + p_2|^2) = T(|p_1|^2 + |p_2|^2) = T(|p_1|^2) + T(|p_2|^2).
$$

This is the Cauchy functional equation $f(a + b) = f(a) + f(b)$ on the non-negative reals — the same equation that appears in Paper #1's derivation of the squared-modulus identity and in Paper #2's Cauchy/Minkowski argument for the Born-rule exponent.

The unique continuous non-negative solution is $T(|p|^2) = c\,|p|^2$ for some constant $c$. Combined with the Galilean integration (§7.4), $c = 1/(2m)$.

### A.2 The integration-Jacobian argument

The Galilean Lie algebra commutator $[\hat{H}, \hat{K}_i] = -i\hbar\hat{p}_i$ with $\hat{K}_i = m\hat{x}_i - t\hat{p}_i$, when applied to the substrate-level bandwidth-flow structure, yields
$$
\partial_{\hat{p}_i}\hat{T} = \frac{\hat{p}_i}{m},
$$
which integrates to
$$
\hat{T} = \frac{|\hat{p}|^2}{2m} + \mathrm{const},
$$
with the constant absorbed into the zero of energy. The factor of $1/2$ is the integration Jacobian (antiderivative of $p$ is $p^2/2$). This is the same Jacobian as Paper #6 §7.4 + Appendix A.1, arrived at here via adjacency-bandwidth-flow Galilean covariance rather than direct Hilbert-space Galilean closure.
