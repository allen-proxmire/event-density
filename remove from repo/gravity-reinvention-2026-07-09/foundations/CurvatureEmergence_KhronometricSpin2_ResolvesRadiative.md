## 1. The kinematic question, settled: the metric carries a spin-2 channel

My earlier "scalar-only radiation" rested on the claim that the emergent metric perturbation is always `h_ij ~ δ_ij` (pure trace / conformal), with zero transverse-traceless (spin-2) part. The probe shows that is the **isotropic special case**, not the general rule.

Measuring the emergent metric via direction-dependent hop-distance on a lattice:

| source | `g_xx` | `g_yy` | trace (spin-0) | traceless (spin-2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| isotropic (point mass) | 0.0496 | 0.0496 | 0.0496 | **0.0000** |
| quadrupolar (binary-like) | 0.0539 | 0.0436 | 0.0488 | **+0.0052** |
| stronger quadrupole | 0.0561 | 0.0382 | 0.0471 | **+0.0090** |

An isotropic source gives a purely conformal metric (`g_xx = g_yy`, spin-2 part zero), the point-mass case that motivated "conformal, scalar only." But a **non-spherical** source makes the bandwidth, and hence the reach, anisotropic, and anisotropic reach gives `g_xx ≠ g_yy`, a genuine **traceless (spin-2)** metric perturbation, scaling with the source anisotropy. **The emergent metric is not locked to conformal; the spin-2 channel is kinematically available.** A binary carries a time-varying quadrupole, so it sources a time-varying spin-2 metric perturbation, the raw material of tensor radiation.
