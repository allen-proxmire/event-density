## 1. Why ED cannot have GR's tensor radiation

GR's gravitational waves are transverse-traceless (spin-2) perturbations of the metric, with two polarizations (`+`, `×`). They exist because in GR the metric is a **dynamical field**: `g_μν` has ten components, and after gauge and constraints, two are radiative degrees of freedom that propagate and carry energy. The metric sourcing itself (the Einstein equation) is what makes those two tensor modes dynamical.

ED's metric is **kinematic**. It is a read-out `g_ij ~ (1/b)δ_ij` of the single scalar bandwidth field `b`; the dynamics live in `b`, and the metric is its shadow (the acoustic-metric class, Paper E §6). So the radiative degrees of freedom are those of the field that actually propagates, `b`, which is **one scalar**. There is no dynamical tensor field, so there are no dynamical tensor modes. The count is exact: ED has one radiative degree of freedom (scalar), where GR has two (tensor).

Concretely, the metric perturbation from any source is
$$h_{ij} = \delta\!\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)\delta_{ij} = -\frac{\delta b}{b^2}\,\delta_{ij} \;\propto\; \delta_{ij}.$$
A perturbation proportional to `δ_ij` is **pure trace** (conformal): it has zero transverse-traceless part. In polarization language it is the **breathing** mode (an isotropic transverse stretch), spin-0, with no `+` or `×` content. The probe confirms it exactly: decomposing the transverse perturbation, ED is `+ = 0.000, × = 0.000, breathing = 1.000`; a GR tensor wave is the reverse, `breathing = 0.000`. **ED radiation is 100% scalar breathing; GR is 100% tensor.**
