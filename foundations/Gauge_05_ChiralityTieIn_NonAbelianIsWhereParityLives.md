## 2. The real result: parity violation is a non-abelian phenomenon

Put the kernel facts together:

- The **abelian** force is the **single-channel / V1** coupling, and V1 is **chirality-blind** → the abelian coupling is the same on both handednesses → **vector → parity-conserving.**
- The **non-abelian** force is the **cross-channel / V5** coupling, and V5 is **chirality-sensitive** → the non-abelian coupling *can* distinguish handedness → **can be chiral → can violate parity.**

> **So in ED, parity violation can only occur in a non-abelian (V5/cross-channel) force. An abelian force is necessarily vector — it cannot violate parity.**

This is a genuine, falsifiable structural prediction (not a theorem of general gauge theory — chiral abelian U(1)s are mathematically allowed; ED forbids them, because the abelian coupling is the chirality-blind V1 kernel). And it matches reality at low energy: **electromagnetism (abelian) is vector; the weak force (non-abelian) is chiral.** The vector/chiral split of the forces is the V1/V5 = single/cross-channel = abelian/non-abelian split. That is the payoff the #2b thread was reaching for, now placed in the gauge structure: V5 is the chirality-bearing kernel *because* it is the non-abelian (cross-channel) one.

## 3. Two honest reasons it is not the full weak force

The result is "parity violation *lives in* the non-abelian sector," not "the weak SU(2) *is* chiral." Two gaps keep it from the latter:

1. **Not every non-abelian force is chiral.** QCD's SU(3) is non-abelian (cross-channel/V5) yet **vector** — the strong force conserves parity. So "non-abelian → chiral" is too strong. The SM reason: quarks couple to SU(3) as L+R (both handedness, symmetric → vector), but to SU(2) as L-only (one handedness, asymmetric → chiral). **Chirality is set by the matter *handedness-assignment* (which channel-topology classes the force couples), not by the group.** ED inherits exactly this: V5 *can* be chiral, but whether a given V5 force *is* depends on whether it couples handedness-asymmetric channels (weak) or symmetric ones (strong).
2. **C-type vs P-type (the open C/P-link).** V5 is *established* sensitive to the **C-type** chirality χ_C (matter/antimatter, R4). The weak force's parity violation is **P-type** (helicity). From SQ1h/SQ1j these are distinct, and whether V5's χ_C-sensitivity extends to helicity-sensitivity is the open C/P-link. So the tie-in's chiral channel is solid for C-type and *conjectural* for the P-type the weak force actually needs.
