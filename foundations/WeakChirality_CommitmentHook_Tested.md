# Testing the "Weak = Commitment-Coupled" Hook — The Simple Version Is Refuted; the Better Version Bottoms Out on Hard SM Physics

**Foundations — pressure-tests the riskiest step in `FirstArrival_HandednessImprint` §6.2: "the weak force is chiral because it is the commitment-coupled / flavor-changing force." Tested before committing it to the paper. Result: **the simple hook is refuted** — the weak *neutral* current (the Z) is chiral but flavor-*conserving*, so chirality is not tied to flavor-change/commitment; chirality is the left-vs-right coupling asymmetry (the matter assignment), independent of whether identity changes. A better version — "the weak force is the uniquely T/CP-violating force, hence the one coupled to the arrow" — is a real correspondence but bottoms out on hard, unaddressed SM physics (the strong-CP problem; the CKM-phase origin of CP violation), and fails to distinguish weak from strong at the kernel level (both are V5/non-abelian, both carry the retarded arrow). A third version — the 2-channel family pairs one-handedly by channel-topology compatibility — is a candidate but unbuilt (it needs the open channel-topology framework). **Conclusion: do not put the commitment-coupled mechanism in the paper. The weak-specific chirality (why left-handed doublets) is genuinely open; the first-arrival centerpiece stands independently — it supplies the handedness *reference*, not the weak-specific *assignment*.****

---

## 1. The hook under test

`FirstArrival` §6.2 offered: the weak force is chiral because it is **commitment-coupled** — it changes flavor/identity (a channel-*transition*, commitment-like), so it couples *at* commitments and inherits the arrow's handedness; the strong force is **transport-coupled** (binding/persistence), hence vector. Phenomenological hook: weak = decay/flavor-change; strong = binding.

## 2. Refutation: the neutral current is chiral but flavor-conserving

The decisive test against the real weak force:

- The **weak neutral current** (the Z boson) is **chiral** — the Z couples to left- and right-handed fermions with *different* strengths (`g ∝ T₃ − Q sin²θ_W`, and `T₃` differs for L and R). Parity is violated in neutral-current processes.
- But the Z is **flavor-conserving** — neutral currents do not change flavor (no tree-level FCNC; GIM mechanism). No identity change, no "commitment."

So here is a coupling that is **chiral without being flavor-changing.** Chirality and flavor-change come apart. **Chirality is the left-vs-right coupling asymmetry (the matter assignment — which handedness is charged), not the flavor-change/commitment.** The simple hook conflated the two; the Z refutes it. (The charged current's flavor-change is a *consequence* of the W being charged within a doublet — a separate fact from the chirality.)

The hook is refuted as stated. This is the value of testing before the paper: the offered mechanism is wrong, the way the spatial-dimension bound was a category error.

## 3. A better version — and why it also doesn't close

**Steelman:** the weak force is the *only* force that violates P, C, CP, and T. The arrow is ED's T-asymmetry primitive. So perhaps the weak force is chiral because it is *the force coupled to the arrow* (the T-violating one), and EM/strong are vector because they are T-symmetric. This is more precise than the flavor-change version — it rests on a real fact (weak is uniquely T/CP-violating). But it fails three ways:

1. **It does not distinguish weak from strong at the kernel level.** Both weak (SU(2)) and strong (SU(3)) are non-abelian = V5 = cross-channel, and V5 is *retarded* (carries the arrow). So "couples to the arrow via V5" is true of *both* — it cannot be why only the weak is chiral.
2. **The strong-CP problem.** The strong force *could* violate CP (the θ-term) but apparently does not (θ ≈ 0) — an unexplained SM puzzle. So "strong is T-symmetric, therefore vector" is not a clean fact ED can lean on; *why* the strong force does not see the arrow (θ = 0) is itself open.
3. **CP violation's origin is the CKM phase** (needing 3 generations, Kobayashi–Maskawa) — a feature of the *matter mixing*, not of the gauge kernel. ED has no account of the CKM phase. So "weak couples to the arrow" bottoms out on matter-sector physics ED does not address.

The T/CP version is a real *direction* but reaches straight into the hardest open problems of the SM (strong-CP, CKM). It is not paper-ready.

## 4. A third version — unbuilt

The chirality is set by the matter assignment (which handedness the force couples). The first-arrival imprint supplies the global handedness *reference*; the open question is why the **2-channel (weak)** family couples to only *one* handedness-class while the **3-channel (strong)** couples to *both*. A candidate: the channel-*topology* of the 2-channel family is compatible with only one chirality-class partner, the 3-channel with both. This is internally sensible but **unbuilt** — it needs the channel-topology framework that is itself the open gauge program. Not paper-ready.

## 5. Verdict and the consequence for the paper

**The simple commitment-coupled hook is refuted (the chiral, flavor-conserving Z); the better T/CP version bottoms out on the strong-CP problem and the CKM phase and fails to distinguish weak from strong at the kernel level; the channel-topology version is unbuilt.** So **the weak-specific chirality — why left-handed doublets feel the weak force and right-handed singlets do not — is genuinely open in ED.** None of the three candidate mechanisms survives as a paper claim.

**What stands, unaffected:** the first-arrival centerpiece supplies the handedness *reference* (the global L/R and matter/antimatter references imprinted by the first commitment, locked by R4, maximal because topological, unifying parity violation with baryogenesis). It does *not* depend on the weak-specific assignment — it says *where chirality comes from* (the arrow), not *why the weak force in particular* is the chiral one. The latter is the marked frontier.

**For the paper:** do not offer the commitment-coupled mechanism. State the weak-specific chirality as genuinely open, with the matter-assignment / channel-topology-compatibility as the candidate direction and the T/CP-violation connection (and its collision with strong-CP/CKM) noted honestly. This *strengthens* the paper's credibility — it draws the line cleanly between what ED accounts for (the handedness reference and its origin in the arrow; parity violation confined to the non-abelian sector) and what it does not yet (the specific weak assignment, tangled with the SM's own hardest puzzles).
