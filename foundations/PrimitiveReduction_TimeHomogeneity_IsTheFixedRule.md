## 1. The two definitions (canonical Paper_087 §5)

- **P03 — "Channel and locus indexing; spatial homogeneity."** Discrete index sets (channels, loci) + translation-invariance of the locus index: "the participation graph is spatially homogeneous; no locus is privileged." So P03 bundles *indexing* + *spatial homogeneity*.
- **P13 — "Time homogeneity."** "The substrate-level laws at time `t` are identical to those at `t'`. No moment is privileged. `∂_t` is a substrate-level symmetry."

Paper_087 §8.4 flags the reduction hypothesis explicitly: *"P13 derivable from P11 + P03 would reduce the primitive count."*

## 2. Part 1 — P03 and P13 do NOT merge into one homogeneity

The tempting move: "space is homogeneous, time is homogeneous, so there is one spacetime-translation symmetry" (the Poincaré/Noether reading). **ED must refuse this**, and refusing it is a distinctive, honest result:

- The reduction audit established that in ED **time carries the arrow (P11) and space does not**. P13 (time-homogeneity, no privileged *moment*) is complementary to P11 (the privileged *direction*); Paper_087 §P13 itself says P13 "ensures P11's commitment-direction is the only temporal-asymmetry primitive." Space has no such directional partner — there is no spatial arrow.
- So "homogeneity of space" and "homogeneity of time" are homogeneities of two structurally **different** arena-axes (one arrowed, one not). They are not one symmetry wearing two hats. Merging them into a single spacetime-translation primitive would import exactly the space-time symmetry ED breaks (the arrow).
- The apparent unification (a single Poincaré translation group in the continuum) is therefore **emergent / coarse-grained**, not primitive — consistent with the standing result that continuum PDEs carry *more* symmetry than the substrate (reversibility and full spacetime symmetry are coarse-graining artifacts). ED's substrate has less symmetry than its continuum limit, by design, because of the arrow.

**So P03 and P13 are not one primitive.** That is the crank-safe answer, and it is ED-distinctive: ED does not have a unified spacetime-homogeneity at the substrate level.

## 3. Part 2 — but P13 reduces to a facet of P11 (sharper than P11 + P03)

The merge fails, but P13 still is not an independent *arena* primitive. It reduces — to the arrow — and the route is cleaner than the paper's "P11 + P03" guess (P03 is not needed):

1. **P11 is the sole process** (reduction audit): all dynamics — every update, every law-application — is commitment. Nothing else generates time-evolution.
2. **Canonical P11 is ONE fixed rule**, not a time-indexed family: "at commitment events, participation collapses to single-channel, un-selected phases randomized, irreversibly." There is no `t` in the rule; it is a single operation.
3. Iterating **one fixed rule** produces dynamics whose laws at `t` and `t'` are identical — there is no room for a privileged moment, because a privileged moment would require the rule to differ there, and there is only one rule.

Therefore **P13 (time-homogeneity of the laws) = "the one process is time-fixed," which is already contained in P11 being a single rule.** P13 is a facet of P11, not a separate stage-fact. And note it needs *only* P11, not P03 — so the sharper statement is `P13 ⊂ P11`, improving on Paper_087's `P13 = P11 + P03` hypothesis (the spatial-homogeneity input is a red herring for the temporal reduction).

**Honest caveat (the one boundary).** "No moment privileged" (P13) is about the *laws*, not the *state*. The arrow may give time a special *initial* moment (a first commitment) — but a special initial **state** is a boundary condition, not a law-inhomogeneity; the laws are still the same everywhere. So the arrow's possible "beginning" is a separate initial-condition matter and does not threaten `P13 ⊂ P11` (which is about law-homogeneity). This is exactly the right scope: P13 as stated is law-invariance, and law-invariance is the fixedness of the one rule.
