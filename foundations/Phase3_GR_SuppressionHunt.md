## 2. The standard field-theory rescues — none apply to ED

How small IR Lorentz violation is achieved in the literature, and why each is unavailable here:

- **Supersymmetry** (SUSY suppresses LV operators): ED has no supersymmetry. *Unavailable.*
- **A low-scale breaking sector** (`f ≪ M_P` by construction, the LV field living at a sub-Planck scale): in ED the breaking *is* the arrow (P11/P13), a **fundamental substrate primitive** — it operates at the Planck/substrate scale by definition. - **Emergent Lorentz invariance** (the LV couplings are *irrelevant* and flow to zero in the IR): the khronometric `c_i` are **marginal** (dimensionless), so they do **not** flow to zero — there is no RG suppression. *Unavailable.*
- **Accidental/custodial symmetry** (an extra symmetry forbidding the LV term): none is found in ED's primitive set beyond the GR-limit one, which gives only stability (the protection hunt), not suppression. *Not found.*

So the textbook routes to soft breaking are closed for ED, essentially because the breaking is *fundamental and marginal*.

## 3. The one ED-specific candidate — commitment sparsity

There is a genuinely ED-specific possibility the standard analysis misses. The khronon (foliation) exists *only* because of the arrow: no commitment ⟹ no preferred time ⟹ GR. So the khronon's stiffness `λ` is set by the **density of commitment (determination) events** in the background, not by the substrate geometry (which sets `M_P²` via P02 sharing, independent of the arrow). If the two have a hierarchy, `λ = (\text{khronon stiffness})/M_P²` could be small:

> **Candidate:** if background (vacuum) commitments are *sparse* — far below the Planck density — while the metric stiffness `M_P²` is set by the (Planck-dense) substrate connectivity, then `f ≪ M_P` and `λ ≪ 1`, suppressing `α₁`.

This is attractive because it is ED-native: commitment is *determination* (a decoherence-like event), and in a coherent vacuum determination could be rare. P11 (commitment) and P13 (the tick) are **separate** primitives, so commitments need not fire every tick everywhere — leaving room for sparsity.

## 4. Why the candidate is disfavored (the honest failure analysis)

Three considerations weigh against it; together they make it unlikely, not impossible:

1. **ED's ontology says becoming is fundamental and ongoing.** The program's core posit is that commitment is *the* fundamental act and the universe is *continuously* becoming. That is the opposite of sparse: it suggests commitment is Planck-dense (the substrate commits everywhere, every tick), giving `f ∼ M_P`, `λ = O(1)`. The candidate requires reading ED *against* its own central commitment.
2. **The gravity sector has dense commitments.** `κ = k_{11}c/ℓ_P` is a *Planck-rate* sink (`κ ∼ c/ℓ_P`), and `κ/D = 8πG` with `k_{11}/s_{02} = O(1)` (the `κ,D` pinning) means the commitment factor is `O(1)`, not small. Near matter, commitments fire at the Planck rate. So where `α₁` is actually measured (the Solar System, near the Sun), commitments are dense — and `α₁` is a *constant* in the action, not a vacuum-only quantity, so the dense near-matter sector is the relevant one.
3. **`λ` is a dimensionless ratio.** Even granting sparse vacuum commitments, `λ = c₂` is khronon-stiffness *over* `M_P²`. For it to be small, the khronon and metric stiffnesses must have a *hierarchy*. Both are Planck-scale band-fraction quantities with `k_{11} ∼ s_{02}` (gravity sector); a ratio of two comparable Planck-scale stiffnesses is `O(1)`, not small. The candidate needs `λ` to be *sub-leading* in the band-fractions, for which no reason is found.

So the sparsity candidate, examined honestly, is **disfavored**: it requires reading ED against its ongoing-becoming character, it conflicts with the dense near-matter commitment sector where `α₁` is measured, and it needs an unexplained hierarchy in a dimensionless ratio. It is not *excluded* — a substrate-specific mechanism making `λ` sub-leading could exist and has not been ruled out — but it is not favored.
