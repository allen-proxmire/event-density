- **Observed:** $\rho_\Lambda \sim (2.3 \times 10^{-3} \text{ eV})^4 \sim 10^{-47}$ GeV⁴, equivalently $\Lambda \sim 10^{-122}$ in Planck units.

Standard physics has no clean derivation. Proposed solutions: anthropic / multiverse arguments; SUSY cancellations (don't quite work); fine-tuning (the corpus's discipline rejects fine-tuning); modified gravity (large-distance modifications); cosmological-constant-as-dynamical-field (quintessence, etc.).

## §2 Candidate substrate-graph quantities for $\Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}$

Four candidates for "what substrate-side quantity corresponds to $\Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}$":

**(i) Substrate-action density $S_{\mathrm{sub}}/V$ in the late-universe regime.** The late-universe Λ-dominated regime corresponds to a substrate state where substrate event-density has dropped dramatically (universe has expanded), most chains are in standard individuation regime (post-saturation), and residual substrate-action density gives the cosmological-constant contribution. **Per M3-chain template:** late-universe $\rho_\Lambda^{\mathrm{eff}}$ = residual substrate-action density after dilution.

**(ii) DCGT coarse-grained vacuum-energy contributions.** DCGT translates substrate-side stress-energy to continuum stress-energy. The continuum-side vacuum-energy contribution depends on what DCGT picks up from substrate-side content at large coarse-graining scales $R_{cg}$. **Suppression mechanism candidate:** if DCGT's coarse-graining at $R_{cg} \sim 1/H_0$ (cosmological scale) systematically suppresses substrate-side high-energy modes, the continuum vacuum-energy density could be naturally small without naive QFT mode-summing.

The asymptotic state's substrate-action density may be intrinsically small (relaxation produces small residual content). **Substrate-side mechanism candidate:** late-time $\Psi$ asymptotic content is small by substrate-graph relaxation dynamics.

**(iv) V1/V5 kernel contributions to late-time effective stress-energy.** V1 + V5 kernels supply substrate-side dynamics; their contributions to the substrate-action density in the late-time regime determine the residual $\rho_\Lambda^{\mathrm{eff}}$. **Substrate-side mechanism candidate:** V1/V5 long-time asymptotics produce naturally-small residual stress-energy.

These candidates overlap — they all touch on "what's the substrate-side state in the late-time regime" — but emphasize different mechanisms (state characterization, coarse-graining suppression, dynamic relaxation, kernel asymptotics).

Load-bearing requirement: substrate-graph quantitative derivation of late-time $S_{\mathrm{sub}}/V$ value from substrate parameters + dilution dynamics. Substrate-research-frontier.

**Route L2 — DCGT suppression of vacuum-energy contributions.**

Mechanism: DCGT coarse-graining at cosmological scale $R_{cg} \sim 1/H_0$ systematically suppresses substrate-side contributions from short-scale modes (V1 finite-width $\ell_{V_1}$ much smaller than $R_{cg}$). But: naive scaling of suppression factors gives at most exponential/polynomial corrections, not 120-order suppression. **Whether DCGT's specific coarse-graining structure produces the right magnitude requires substrate-research-frontier work.**

Load-bearing requirement: substrate-graph derivation of DCGT's suppression factors for vacuum-energy contributions at cosmological scales. Substrate-research-frontier.

Mechanism: post-saturation $\Psi$ relaxes from saturation level (where $\rho_{\mathrm{sat}}$ is GUT-scale) to long-time asymptotic level. The relaxation involves exponential decay over cosmic-time scales; the asymptotic residual is exponentially small.

But: standard inflation reheating produces a hot Big Bang, not a slowly-relaxing universe. The "relaxation" in standard cosmology is the universe's expansion (volume dilution), not field-theoretic relaxation.

Load-bearing requirement: substrate-graph derivation of $\Psi$ relaxation dynamics in post-saturation regime + connection to observed $\rho_\Lambda$ value. Substrate-research-frontier.

**Route L4 — Friedmann inheritance + substrate-side boundary conditions.**
