## 1. Framing

### 1.1 What commitment irreversibility means in the ED substrate

A **commitment event** at substrate locus $u$ is a discrete primitive event at which a chain's participation across multiple channels $\mathcal{K}(u)$ collapses to a single channel $K^*$. Before commitment, the chain participates coherently across $|\mathcal{K}(u)|$ channels with bandwidth $\{b_K(u)\}$ and polarity $\{\pi_K(u)\}$ distributed across the available-channel set. After commitment, the chain participates only in $K^*$, with bandwidth fully transferred and the other channels' content extinguished. The selection rule for $K^*$ — the bandwidth-fraction Born-rule pattern — is downstream content (Paper #2); the present paper concerns only what *happens to* the pre-commitment content after commitment occurs.

**Commitment irreversibility** says: there is no substrate-level dynamics that maps the post-commitment state back to a coherent superposition resembling the pre-commitment state. Specifically:

- The pre-commitment phase relationships $\{\pi_K(u) : K \in \mathcal{K}(u), K \neq K^*\}$ are *non-recoverable*. After commitment, the substrate retains no structural record of these phases sufficient to reconstruct the original superposition.
- The pre-commitment bandwidth allocations $\{b_K(u) : K \in \mathcal{K}(u), K \neq K^*\}$ are likewise non-recoverable: the bandwidth that was in non-selected channels has been transferred and re-allocated; no substrate-level operation reverses this transfer.
- The "direction" of bandwidth flow during commitment is fixed: it flows *from* the available-channel set *into* $K^*$, never the reverse.

Irreversibility is therefore a *structural asymmetry* at the substrate level — a property of how the substrate's commitment operation maps states, not an external thermodynamic statement.

### 1.2 Why P11 irreversibility is load-bearing across the program

The irreversibility component of P11 plays a structural role in three program-level loci:

**Kernel rule-types (V1, V5) inherit retarded support from P11.** The downstream argument (formalized in Paper #19 as Theorem T18) is: V1 cannot admit advanced support because advanced V1 would require chain contributions propagating from future commitment events back toward past pre-commitment states — but post-commitment states have no path back to pre-commitment states under P11 irreversibility. Symmetric V1 is non-constructible for the same reason: the symmetric support would require backward-chain contributions, which the substrate has no mechanism to produce. Only retarded V1 is structurally consistent with irreversible commitment.

**Horizons form as one-way decoupling surfaces.** A horizon at the substrate level is a locus where cross-bandwidth $\Gamma_\mathrm{cross}$ collapses below hydrodynamic-window resolution. For the collapse to constitute a *horizon* — a one-way information barrier — the bandwidth flow across the surface must be asymmetric: information can flow out (toward the future, away from the horizon) but not back across (toward the past). ## 4. Key Vocabulary

For the reader new to ED and to the M-series:

- **Commitment.** A discrete substrate-level event at substrate locus $u$ at which a chain's multi-channel participation across the available-channel set $\mathcal{K}(u)$ collapses to a single channel $K^*$. Commitment is the substrate-level mechanism producing discrete measurement outcomes empirically observed in laboratory experiments.

- **Commitment event.** A specific instance of commitment, characterized by: a chain, a substrate locus $u$, the pre-commitment available-channel set $\mathcal{K}(u)$, the selected channel $K^* \in \mathcal{K}(u)$, and the pre-and-post-commitment bandwidth distributions.

- **Irreversible commitment.** Commitment whose post-state cannot be transformed back to its pre-state by any substrate-level dynamics. Equivalently: the substrate-level commitment operation has no inverse.

- **Adjacency-preserving propagation.** A property of substrate-level dynamical operations: they map adjacent loci to adjacent loci, preserving the participation-graph's edge structure. Equivalently: no "teleportation" of bandwidth between non-adjacent loci is permitted at the primitive substrate level.

- **Kernel inheritance.** The structural property by which substrate-level kernel rule-types (V1, V5, etc.) inherit causal-direction structure from substrate primitives. Specifically: P11 irreversibility produces retarded support in V1 (downstream content of Paper #19); the substrate-level mechanism for this inheritance is described abstractly at the meta-substrate level in §7.C below.

- **Monotonic information flow.** Information-theoretic monotonicity: that information content (Shannon entropy of relevant distributions) cannot increase under substrate-level dynamics applied locally. Equivalent to data processing inequality at the substrate level.

- **Compositional closure.** The structural requirement that the substrate's primitive operations compose consistently — operations chain together to produce new operations within the same operational class. For commitment specifically: commitment must compose with substrate-level propagation in a way producing a well-defined substrate-level operation (no contradiction, no ambiguity).

- **Symmetry-breaking.** A substrate-level operation breaks a symmetry if the post-operation state has lower symmetry than the pre-operation state. Commitment breaks the permutation symmetry across $\mathcal{K}(u)$: pre-commitment, all channels are symmetric (in the sense of being structurally interchangeable by relabeling); post-commitment, $K^*$ is structurally distinguished from the rest.

- **Causal monotonicity.** The property that the substrate's dynamical operations advance information forward in time (or, more precisely, along the substrate's primitive participation-graph causal direction), never backward. Causal monotonicity at the substrate level supplies the foundation for the empirically observed causal direction of physics.

- **Horizon-formation constraint.** The structural condition that substrate-level decoupling surfaces (where cross-bandwidth $\Gamma_\mathrm{cross}$ collapses) form as *one-way* information barriers. This condition is incompatible with reversible commitment: a reversible commitment process across a candidate horizon would prevent the surface from being a one-way barrier.

- **Meta-substrate $\{C^*\}$.** Framework within which M-series forcing arguments operate (Meta-Paper M0 §5).

- **Forcing routes.** The five candidate routes for upstream forcing of substrate primitives: A (symmetry-minimality / symmetry-breaking), B (operational adequacy / information-theoretic monotonicity), C (compositional closure / kernel inheritance), D (adjacency-preserving propagation / category-theoretic), and E (horizon-formation / structural-cosmological constraints).

## 6. Alternative Encodings $\{A1-A6\}$ and $\{B1-B6\}$

This section enumerates structural alternatives to P11 irreversibility. Exclusion arguments appear in §8.

### 6.A. A-series — Structural alternatives within the substrate-ontology framework

**A1. Fully reversible commitment.**
A commitment operation that has a structural inverse: there exists a substrate-level dynamics mapping any post-commitment state back to its pre-commitment coherent superposition. Mathematically: commitment is a bijection on substrate states.

**A2. Partially reversible commitment.**
A commitment operation that is reversible on some subset of substrate states (e.g., commitment events with $|\mathcal{K}(u)| = 2$ are reversible, but events with $|\mathcal{K}(u)| \geq 3$ are irreversible). Mathematically: commitment is an injection but not a bijection, with the image being a proper subset of pre-commitment states.

**A3. Symmetric commitment (no arrow).**
Commitment that produces equal substrate-level structural content in both temporal directions. Mathematically: pre-commitment and post-commitment states are related by a symmetry that preserves substrate observables — there is no structurally-distinguished "before" and "after."

**A4. Stochastic reversible commitment.**
Commitment that is irreversible at the level of individual events but reversible on average across an ensemble of events. Mathematically: while no individual commitment has an inverse, the *expectation* of the commitment operation over a substrate ensemble has a well-defined inverse.

**A5. Commitment without adjacency preservation.**
Commitment that respects irreversibility locally at locus $u$ but produces non-local bandwidth flows: post-commitment bandwidth re-allocations propagate instantaneously to non-adjacent loci. Mathematically: commitment has discontinuous support on the participation graph.

**A6. Commitment without information-theoretic monotonicity.**
Commitment that is in some sense "irreversible" (no exact inverse) but does not produce monotonic decrease of pre-commitment substrate information. E.g., commitment that loses some information but gains others, in a structurally balanced way. Mathematically: commitment's pre-image-information is not monotonically smaller than its image-information.

### 6.B. B-series — Mainstream alternatives from physics frameworks

**B1. Classical reversible dynamics.**
Hamiltonian classical mechanics with no measurement-event mechanism — pure phase-space flow with Liouville's theorem holding strictly. The "measurement-like" events of empirical physics are reversible coarse-grainings under this framework.

**B2. Hamiltonian reversible flow.**
Quantum unitary evolution without collapse — only $U_t = e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}$ acts on states. Measurement appears only as the relative-state structure of decoherence theory; no fundamental irreversible operation.

**B3. Liouville-preserving reversible evolution.**
Liouville-equation-conserving dynamics on classical phase space, with no collapse mechanism. Statistical-mechanical entropy increase is a coarse-graining artifact, not a substrate-level structural fact.

**B4. Reversible Markov processes.**
Markov-chain dynamics where the transition probabilities satisfy detailed balance, ensuring time-reversibility at the ensemble level. Specific implementations: detailed-balance equilibrium statistical mechanics, certain reversible computing models.

**B5. Reversible field-operator dynamics.**
QFT-level dynamics with only unitary evolution and no measurement-event structure. Vacuum and excitation states evolve reversibly; observable outcomes are accounted-for via decoherence or relative-state arguments.

**B6. Reversible stochastic thermodynamics.**
Stochastic thermodynamic frameworks (Crooks fluctuation theorem, Jarzynski equality) in which entropy production fluctuations satisfy detailed-balance-like reversibility at the trajectory level. Macroscopic irreversibility is a statistical consequence, not a structural commitment.

The A-series alternatives are structural variations within the substrate-ontology framework: they accept that the substrate has some kind of commitment-like operation but propose different irreversibility-structures. The B-series alternatives operate from outside the substrate-ontology framework: they propose frameworks that lack a fundamental commitment-operation entirely, with measurement appearing only as an emergent or coarse-grained phenomenon.

## 7. Constructive Necessity

This section derives commitment irreversibility from $\{C^*\}$ + M-1 + M-2 + background mathematics via five independent forcing routes.

### 7.A. Route A: Symmetry-Breaking

Pre-commitment, the substrate at locus $u$ has a chain participating across the available-channel set $\mathcal{K}(u)$ with bandwidth and polarity distributed across all channels. The available-channel set has a structural permutation symmetry $S_{|\mathcal{K}(u)|}$ — under relabeling of channels (with appropriate bandwidth and polarity transport), the substrate's primitive structure is invariant.

A commitment event selects a single channel $K^* \in \mathcal{K}(u)$. This selection *breaks* the $S_{|\mathcal{K}(u)|}$ permutation symmetry: post-commitment, $K^*$ is structurally distinguished from the other channels.

For the commitment to be *reversible*, the substrate would need a dynamical operation that restores the broken symmetry — i.e., maps the post-commitment state (in which $K^*$ is distinguished) back to a state in which all channels are again structurally symmetric. But the substrate's primitive structure has *no mechanism* to restore broken symmetry without external information about which channel was selected and what the original pre-commitment phases were.

The pre-commitment phases $\{\pi_K(u) : K \in \mathcal{K}(u)\}$ are stored in the substrate's $U(1)$ polarity structure (M-1). Post-commitment, the polarity content for channels $K \neq K^*$ is extinguished as bandwidth flows into $K^*$ (per the four-band partition structure of M-2: commitment-reserve band $b^\mathrm{com}$ acts as the structural locus for the bandwidth transfer). The phase information that *was* in the polarity structure of $K \neq K^*$ is therefore non-recoverable from post-commitment substrate content alone.

**Conclusion of Route A.** Commitment breaks the $S_{|\mathcal{K}(u)|}$ permutation symmetry; no substrate-level operation restores the broken symmetry; therefore commitment is irreversible.

### 7.B. Route B: Information-Theoretic Monotonicity

Pre-commitment, the substrate at locus $u$ carries information $I_\mathrm{pre}$ characterizing the chain's participation distribution: bandwidth fractions $\{b_K(u)/\sum_{K'} b_{K'}(u)\}$, polarity content $\{\pi_K(u)\}$, and the channel-set $\mathcal{K}(u)$. The Shannon entropy of the pre-commitment bandwidth-fraction distribution is

$$H_\mathrm{pre} = -\sum_{K \in \mathcal{K}(u)} (b_K/B) \log(b_K/B), \quad B = \sum_K b_K.$$

Post-commitment, the substrate carries only the information that $K^*$ was selected and that bandwidth has been transferred to it. The post-commitment Shannon entropy of the bandwidth-fraction distribution is

$$H_\mathrm{post} = 0$$

(concentrated on $K^*$). The information loss is $\Delta I = H_\mathrm{pre} - H_\mathrm{post} = H_\mathrm{pre} > 0$.

For commitment to be reversible, the substrate would need a dynamical operation that restores $H_\mathrm{pre}$ from $H_\mathrm{post}$ — i.e., increases substrate-internal information about pre-commitment bandwidth distribution. But the data processing inequality (a fundamental theorem of information theory) says that local information-processing operations cannot increase mutual information between substrate-internal content and external (pre-commitment) reference content. Equivalently: no Markov chain step can extract information about the past from the present state alone.

The substrate's commitment operation maps a high-entropy pre-commitment state to a zero-entropy post-commitment state, with the lost information *not stored anywhere in the substrate* (per Route A: the polarity content of non-selected channels is extinguished, not transferred elsewhere). The information is irrecoverably gone from substrate-internal content. By the data processing inequality, no substrate operation can restore it.

**Conclusion of Route B.** Commitment produces a monotonic information loss in substrate-internal content that no substrate operation can reverse; therefore commitment is irreversible.

### 7.C. Route C: Compositional Closure / Kernel Inheritance

The substrate's kernel rule-types (V1, V5, and any others) compose with substrate-level propagation operations to produce well-defined kernel-level dynamics. For this composition to be *closed* (per the meta-substrate's compositional-closure requirement, M0 §5 C*5), each kernel must have a definite causal-direction structure that is preserved under composition.

Consider a substrate with reversible commitment (alternative A1). Under reversible commitment, the substrate has a structural symmetry between "pre-commitment → post-commitment" and "post-commitment → pre-commitment" dynamics. This symmetry would extend to the kernel rule-types: V1's vacuum-fluctuation kernel would need to admit *both* retarded support (for forward-causal propagation) *and* advanced support (for backward-causal propagation), since both directions are equally consistent with the substrate's reversible structure.

But the kernel composition operation is then *not closed* — it produces ambiguous results. A two-step chain of kernel applications could compose as (retarded ∘ retarded) producing forward propagation, or (advanced ∘ advanced) producing backward propagation, or (retarded ∘ advanced) producing a kernel with mixed support. The substrate has no structural way to select among these three composition outcomes; the kernel composition is multivalued.

Compositional closure requires the kernel composition to be a single well-defined substrate operation. The only way to achieve this is to *exclude* the advanced-and-mixed-support possibilities by structural fiat — i.e., to commit the substrate to retarded-only support, which requires irreversible commitment to fix the causal direction.

**Conclusion of Route C.** Reversible commitment produces non-closed kernel composition; only irreversible commitment supports compositional-closed kernel rule-types; therefore commitment is irreversible.

### 7.D. Route D: Adjacency-Preserving Propagation

The substrate's primitive participation-graph structure (P03 + P07) supports propagation operations that map adjacent loci to adjacent loci. This adjacency-preserving requirement is a structural commitment of the meta-substrate (C*6).

Under reversible commitment (A1), substrate dynamics would include "post-commitment-to-pre-commitment" operations. These operations would require the substrate to map a chain at locus $u$ at time $t_2 > t_1$ (post-commitment) back to a chain at locus $u$ at time $t_1$ (pre-commitment) — i.e., propagation *backward along the participation-graph causal direction*.

But the participation graph's causal direction is fixed by adjacency: edges connect $(u, t)$ to $(u', t')$ with $t' > t$ (or with $t' = t$ for purely-spatial adjacency, but never with $t' < t$). Backward-causal propagation would require *negative-length edges* in the participation graph — substrate structure that primitive P03 + P07 does not supply.

Equivalently: the substrate's primitive adjacency relations are *forward-causal*. Adjacency-preserving propagation must respect this forward-causal structure. Reversible commitment would require backward-causal propagation, violating adjacency preservation.

**Conclusion of Route D.** Reversible commitment requires backward-causal propagation, which violates the substrate's adjacency-preserving structure; therefore commitment is irreversible.

### 7.E. Route E: Horizon-Formation Constraints

The substrate supports structural loci where cross-bandwidth $\Gamma_\mathrm{cross}$ collapses below hydrodynamic-window resolution. Such loci are *horizons* in the substrate-level structural sense (independent of Paper #10's specific BH derivation, which is downstream): one-way information barriers across which bandwidth-flow is asymmetric.

For a candidate horizon to be a *one-way* barrier — to have empirically-observed horizon character — the bandwidth-flow across it must respect a fixed direction. Information flows outward from the horizon (away from the high-bandwidth-density region) but not back across.

Under reversible commitment, the substrate's commitment operation has no fixed causal direction. Bandwidth flows from $\mathcal{K}(u) \to K^*$ could equally be reversed to $K^* \to \mathcal{K}(u)$. Applied near a candidate horizon, reversible commitment would allow bandwidth-flow to occur in both directions across the horizon surface — preventing the surface from constituting a one-way barrier.

But empirical physics observes horizons (event horizons, cosmological horizons, particle horizons in cosmology) as one-way barriers. The meta-substrate's structural-normative goal C*7 requires the substrate to support these empirically observed horizons. Therefore the substrate's commitment operation must have fixed causal direction — i.e., must be irreversible.

**Conclusion of Route E.** Horizons as one-way information barriers require irreversible commitment; therefore commitment is irreversible.

### 10.3 Glossary extensions (beyond §4)

- **Data processing inequality.** A fundamental theorem of information theory stating that, for any Markov chain $X \to Y \to Z$, mutual information satisfies $I(X; Z) \leq I(X; Y)$. Equivalently: subsequent processing of $Y$ cannot increase information about the original $X$. Used in Route B to establish irreversibility from substrate's information-shedding character at commitment.
- **Detailed balance.** A condition on stochastic dynamics in which transition probabilities satisfy $\pi(x) P(x \to y) = \pi(y) P(y \to x)$ for stationary distribution $\pi$. Markov processes with detailed balance are time-reversible at the ensemble level. Used in B4 exclusion.
- **Permutation symmetry $S_n$.** The symmetric group of permutations on $n$ elements. Substrate-level permutation symmetry across the available-channel set $\mathcal{K}(u)$ is $S_{|\mathcal{K}(u)|}$ if all channels carry equal structural status pre-commitment.
- **Causal direction.** The substrate-level distinction between "before" and "after" in the participation-graph adjacency structure. Forward-causal: from past to future; backward-causal: from future to past. Adjacency-preserving propagation is forward-causal by P03 + P07 structure.
- **Cross-bandwidth $\Gamma_\mathrm{cross}$.** Substrate-level quantity measuring the cross-chain correlation bandwidth between distant chains. Used in horizon-formation analysis: the locus where $\Gamma_\mathrm{cross}$ collapses below hydrodynamic-window resolution is a horizon.
- **Decoupling surface.** A substrate-level locus where bandwidth-flow between chains decouples (drops below hydrodynamic-window threshold). Equivalent to a horizon at the structural level. Empirical correspondence: event horizons in BH physics, cosmological horizons.
