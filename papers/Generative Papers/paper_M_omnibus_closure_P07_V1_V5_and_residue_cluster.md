## 7. Constructive Necessity

### 7.1 Full forcing of P07 (channel structure as ontological primitive)

The substrate must have *some* primitive structure on which P09 polarity (M-1), P04 bandwidth (M-2), and P11 commitment (M-3) act. The candidate structural objects are: loci alone (no channel structure, A1); continuous-manifold channels (A2); or discrete-ontologically-primitive channels (the P07 hypothesis).

**Route C — Compositional closure.**

The substrate's primitive operations (bandwidth-flow between channels, polarity-transport along edges, commitment-event selection from $\mathcal{K}(u)$) require *channels-as-objects* in a monoidal-categorical structure. The categorical composition $K_1 \otimes K_2 \to K_3$ (representing joint-channel composition) is well-defined only when channels carry intrinsic identities distinguishable under composition.

Under alternative A1 (no channel structure), the substrate has no objects to compose; bandwidth and polarity attached directly to loci provide neither the cardinality nor the structural distinguishability required for the substrate's primitive operations. Specifically:

- Commitment events (P11, M-3) select a single $K^* \in \mathcal{K}(u)$ from an available-channel set at locus $u$. Without channels-as-objects, the selection operation has no domain — the substrate has no structurally-distinguishable alternatives to select from.
- Polarity-transport (M-1) transports angular content along edges of the participation graph; "along edges" requires the graph to have distinguishable edges, which in turn requires channels-as-objects.

Under alternative A2 (continuous-manifold channels), the substrate would carry continuous channel-parameter content. The substrate's commitment events would then need to select from an uncountable continuous set — but commitment is a discrete event (M-3), and the substrate's primitive structure under M-3 supports only countable / discrete selection operations. Continuous-manifold channels are operationally inconsistent with M-3.

**Route E — Category-theoretic necessity.**

The Coecke-Kissinger framework (Coecke & Kissinger 2017) classifies substrate-level structural operations as morphisms in a dagger-symmetric-monoidal category. The category requires:

- **Objects:** structurally-distinguishable types that morphisms operate between.
- **Morphisms:** structure-preserving maps between objects.
- **Composition:** sequential and parallel composition of morphisms, satisfying associativity and identity laws.

For the substrate's monoidal-categorical structure to be well-defined under Routes M-1 (polarity-as-action), M-2 (bandwidth-as-additive-scalar), M-3 (commitment-as-collapse), M-4 (symmetry-as-Lie-action), the objects must be channels with intrinsic identities. Alternative A1 (no objects) is categorically inconsistent. Alternative A2 (continuous-manifold objects) is consistent with continuous categories (e.g., the category of measurable spaces) but inconsistent with M-3's discrete-commitment structure.

**Route Adjacency-Inheritance.**

M-3's Route D (adjacency-preserving propagation) and M-4's Route C (adjacency-preserving symmetry action) presuppose a participation-graph structure with discrete adjacency relations. The graph's vertices are loci; the graph's edges connect adjacent loci. The natural objects associated with this graph structure are *channels at vertices* (P07's content). Without channels-as-objects, the adjacency-preserving structure of M-3 + M-4 has no carrier.

**Conclusion of §7.1.** P07 (channel structure as ontological primitive) is **fully forced** under Routes C + E + Adjacency-Inheritance. Discrete-ontologically-primitive channels are the unique structural commitment supporting the M-1 + M-2 + M-3 + M-4 closures' joint operational requirements. Alternatives A1 (no channels) and A2 (continuous-manifold channels) are excluded.
