## 1. The Question

Black holes have produced the most contested set of paradoxes in modern theoretical physics. The information paradox, articulated by Hawking in 1975, asks whether quantum information that falls into a black hole is destroyed when the black hole evaporates — a possibility that would violate the unitarity of quantum mechanics. The firewall paradox of Almheiri, Marolf, Polchinski, and Sully (2012) asks whether an infalling observer must encounter a wall of high-energy radiation at the horizon, contradicting the equivalence principle's prediction that a freely-falling observer should experience nothing locally unusual at the horizon. The complementarity contradiction, due to Susskind and others, attempts to resolve the information paradox by saying information is both inside and outside the horizon depending on observer, but runs into difficulties when entanglement monogamy is enforced at the geometric boundary.

These paradoxes have generated forty years of theoretical literature. They have not been resolved within standard theoretical frameworks. They are real puzzles, taken seriously by serious physicists, and the absence of consensus on their resolution is one of the central open problems in fundamental physics.

The Event Density framework's contribution to black hole physics is structurally different from the standard paradox-resolution programs. The framework does not solve the information paradox. It does not resolve the firewall paradox. It does not arbitrate between complementarity and its alternatives. Instead, it shows that all of these paradoxes are generated by structural assumptions — specifically four of them — that the framework's substrate ontology does not make. Without the assumptions that generate the paradoxes, the paradoxes do not arise.

The four assumptions are:

**Global unitarity** of the full quantum state on a single Hilbert space.

**Global Cauchy data** specifying the entire universe's quantum state on a connected spacelike surface.

**Geometric horizon** as a sharp, primitive boundary in spacetime.

**Monogamy of entanglement** enforced at that geometric boundary.

Standard treatments impose all four. The information paradox arises from combining (i) and (iii): unitarity requires information preservation, but the geometric horizon seems to allow information to fall through and be destroyed. The firewall paradox arises from combining (i), (iii), and (iv): unitarity, the geometric boundary, and monogamy together force a high-energy wall at the horizon. Complementarity attempts to dissolve these by relaxing locality, but the geometric boundary in (iii) makes it hard to specify *where* information is, and monogamy in (iv) keeps producing contradictions.

The framework refuses all four assumptions at the substrate level. There is no global unitarity requirement — the framework is local and irreversible at the substrate level via P11 commitment irreversibility. ## 5. Information Architecture and Evaporation

The third structural result is the substrate-level account of information flow at black holes. The argument runs through the committed-vs-uncommitted distinction, why information cannot cross the horizon, why entanglement can straddle it, the participation-re-routing mechanism for evaporation, and the explicit statement of why the standard paradoxes are not generated.

### 5.1 Information vs entanglement: substrate distinction

The key substrate distinction is between two structurally different forms of correlation:

**Committed structure (information).** A pattern in the substrate that has gone through P11 commitment — participation events have been irreversibly recorded in the local ED-history. Committed structure at point A can only be "read" by point B if there exists a participation pathway from A to B across which substrate events can propagate. This requires nonzero cross-bandwidth $\Gamma_c$ross.

**Uncommitted structure (entanglement).** A correlation potential between two regions — the joint participation-amplitude structure prior to commitment. Uncommitted structure does not require ongoing cross-bandwidth between the two regions. It is set up by the prior participation history that produced the pair, and it persists as a correlation in joint outcomes whenever commitment events occur.

The structural difference:

Committed structure transmission requires nonzero $\Gamma_c$ross at the surface separating sender and receiver.

Entanglement amplitude depends on the correlation structure laid down at pair-formation; once formed, it does not require ongoing cross-bandwidth to persist.

Schematically:

$$
\Gamma_{\mathrm{cross}} \to 0 \implies committed structure blocked
but entanglement amplitude remains finite
$$

In standard QFT terms: a horizon blocks signaling but not pre-existing correlations. In framework terms: a horizon blocks committed structure transmission but not uncommitted structure persistence.

This is not a new claim — it is the substrate translation of the standard quantum-information no-signaling theorem applied at a horizon. What is new in the framework is that both sides — committed and uncommitted — have substrate-level definitions, so the no-signaling-but-correlations-persist behavior is derived rather than postulated.

### 5.2 Why information cannot cross a horizon

From §3, the decoupling surface is defined by exponential cross-bandwidth suppression. The argument:

Committed structure transmission across a surface Σ requires nonzero $\Gamma_c$ross across Σ — without ongoing cross-bandwidth, no participation event on side A can register as a participation event on side B.

At the decoupling surface, $\Gamma_c$ross is below hydrodynamic-window resolution. From the coarse-grained continuum perspective accessible to any external observer, the cross-bandwidth is effectively zero.

Therefore committed structure cannot cross the decoupling surface. Information, in the substrate sense, does not pass through a horizon.

This is not a causal prohibition. The framework has no fundamental light-cone primitive — light cones emerge as level sets of the acoustic metric, which is itself a coarse-grained reading. The prohibition against information crossing the horizon is a participation-bandwidth prohibition: the substrate channels that would carry committed structure have their bandwidth suppressed below resolution.

The distinction matters for two reasons. It does not require any global causal-structure argument; it follows from local bandwidth suppression. And it is consistent with finite-$\Gamma_c$ross tunneling behavior near (but not beyond) the surface — the suppression is exponential, not a hard cutoff. This will be load-bearing for the evaporation mechanism below.
