The framework seeks a Lorentz-scalar, energy-dimensional, amplitude-invariant functional of bandwidth content suitable as a candidate primitive-level mass structure. Six selection criteria constrain the form:

**SC4 — Band-additive.** The four-band decomposition's additive structure must be respected. Different bands contribute additively, weighted by the rule-type-specific band-partition pattern.

### 3.3 Why the logarithmic derivative

The choice of ∂_μ ln b rather than ∂_μ b is the key structural move. The logarithmic derivative implements amplitude-invariance (SC2): for a uniform rescaling b → αb with constant α > 0:

$$
\partial_\mu \ln(\alpha b) = \partial_\mu(\ln \alpha + \ln b) = \partial_\mu \ln b

The constant ln α has zero gradient. The functional is unchanged.

This isolates the spectral rate of variation of b — its structural content — from the amplitude of b — which is chain-specific initial-condition data, not rule-type-structural. The amplitude is set by how much participation bandwidth the chain happens to carry; the spectral rate is set by how that bandwidth varies along the worldline, which is the rule-type's structural fingerprint.

The dimensional analysis works: ∂_μ ln b has units of [length]^(−1) (since ln b is dimensionless and ∂_μ has units of [length]^(−1)). The Lorentz contraction (∂_μ ln b)(∂^μ ln b) has units of [length]^(−2). The square root gives [length]^(−1). Multiplying by ℏc (with c absorbed in the metric signature) gives [energy].
