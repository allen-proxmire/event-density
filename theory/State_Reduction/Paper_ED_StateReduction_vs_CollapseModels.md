## Abstract

There is a serious minority program that says the collapse of the wavefunction is not observer-magic but a real physical effect, and that its cause is **gravity**. Lajos Diósi (1987) got it first: refuse to quantize the gravitational field, treat it instead as fundamentally indeterminate — a universal noise — and that noise decoheres superpositions of different mass distributions on a time `τ ≈ ℏ/E_G`, where `E_G` is the gravitational self-energy of the difference between the two mass configurations. Roger Penrose (1996) gave the deepest version: a superposition of two mass distributions is a superposition of two *spacetimes*, and general covariance makes the notion of "which time is flowing" across them ill-defined, so the very time-translation operator of the superposed state is uncertain; that time-uncertainty *is* an energy uncertainty `E_G`, and the state decays in `τ ≈ ℏ/E_G`. Sabine Hossenfelder (2025) gave the newest: assume matter and geometry are literally the same quantum state with no independent geometric degrees of freedom, note that this is incompatible with unitary evolution, and let the state collapse locally by evolving off the Schrödinger equation — paying for locality with superdeterminism. Event Density belongs in this room, and it makes a claim that is both stronger and different. Where all three say *gravity causes collapse*, ED says measurement's definiteness and gravity's time are **one primitive wearing two hats** — the arrow, which is the irreversible act of commitment: the same arrow that selects the quantum pointer basis (einselection) *is* the khronon, gravity's preferred time. The collapse variable in ED is therefore **time** (the committed arrow), one step upstream of the **energy** `E_G` that Penrose's time-ambiguity produces. This has three consequences. ED *supplies the preferred time Penrose finds missing*, resolving the gap his argument opens rather than competing with it. ED *shares Hossenfelder's matter-equals-geometry ontology* — that is just ED's emergent geometry, `g ~ 1/b` — but obtains locality from the *forward* arrow and its capacity-zero result rather than from her superdeterminism. And ED *dissolves the problem of time* instead of adding a collapse term to Schrödinger. What ED does not yet have is the number: it has not derived a collapse rate, and so cannot say whether it recovers `ℏ/E_G` (already under experimental pressure) or predicts something different. That derivation is the work this note names.

## §1 — The shared conviction

Standard quantum mechanics leaves the collapse of the wavefunction outside its own equations. Unitary evolution is smooth and deterministic; the definite outcome of a measurement is not; and the theory bridges the two only by invoking an "observer" or a "measurement," which is a placeholder, not a mechanism. A minority of physicists have refused that placeholder and asked what actually makes an outcome definite. A striking fraction of them arrive at the same suspect: **gravity.**

The reasoning is not arbitrary. Gravity is the one interaction that couples to *everything* and grows with mass, so it is the natural candidate for a process that leaves microscopic superpositions alone (we see interference for electrons) but destroys macroscopic ones (we never see a cat in two places). And gravity is where quantum mechanics and general relativity are known to be incompatible, so it is where a *new* effect, beyond textbook QM, is most expected.

Diósi, Penrose, Hossenfelder, and Event Density all share two convictions that set them apart from the mainstream:

- **Geometry is not an independent field waiting to be quantized.** This is the quiet radical move. Mainstream quantum gravity quantizes the metric as one more field. Diósi refuses — he treats the Newtonian potential as a classical field with a fundamental indeterminacy. Penrose keeps the geometry semiclassical, so that *the mass distribution is the spacetime*. Hossenfelder goes furthest, making geometry carry no degrees of freedom of its own: matter and geometry are the *same* quantum state. Event Density is on exactly this side of the line, and arguably furthest of all: geometry in ED is **emergent**, `g ~ 1/b`, the coarse-grained shadow of the substrate's participation density, never a primitive field.
- **Collapse is physical, and it is gravity's doing.** Not an update of an observer's knowledge; a real event in the world, rooted in gravity or geometry, that would happen in an empty universe with no observers in it.

Those two commitments are the common ground. The rest of this note is where ED, having entered the room agreeing, then says something the others do not.

## §2 — ED's account of state reduction (cited, not re-derived)

ED's account of measurement predates this comparison and is stated in the Report; here it is only summarized, with its tiers, so the positioning has something concrete to stand on.

**The arrow *is* the collapse (Report §4).** ED has exactly one collapse primitive: **commitment (P11), the irreversible act by which the substrate resolves a participation into a definite channel.** This single primitive delivers the structural facts standard QM leaves unexplained. It selects the **pointer basis**: a superposition is never itself a channel, so committing a superposed state resolves it to one channel or another, never to the superposition — the channel basis is the unique basis in which outcomes are definite, and it is picked out by the arrow, not by environmental decoherence. This is **einselection made primitive rather than derived.** The same irreversibility supplies **repeatability**: a committed channel is locked (an irreversible outcome cannot drift), so re-measurement returns it with certainty — irreversibility is not an obstruction to the quantum-logical covering law, it is what supplies it. And because the bandwidth `b_K` is an intrinsic state property, the **Born probabilities `P(K) = b_K/Σb` are non-contextual**, consistent with Gleason and Kochen–Specker at once because outcomes are *stochastically committed, not pre-valued*. *Tier: a coherent reconstruction of quantum kinematics via the Gleason/Piron–Solèr chain, not a closed theorem; orthogonality is reduced to operational distinguishability and the complex field is selected, with Solèr lattice rigor the standing residual.*

**The same arrow is gravity's time (Report §6).** ED's gravity is khronometric: it is built on a preferred foliation, and that foliation is the arrow made dynamical — the **khronon**. So the preferred time that quantum mechanics wants (to make outcomes definite *at* a time, distinguishing before from after) and the preferred foliation that ED's gravity runs on are **not two structures to be reconciled; they are one primitive, coarse-grained into two sectors.** The "problem of time" — QM wants a preferred time, GR's diffeomorphism invariance forbids one — does not get solved by compromise; it **dissolves**, because ED genuinely has a preferred time (the arrow), and that arrow does both jobs. *Tier: a structural synthesis, not a theorem. The identification is forced by the finding that commitment is the sole process primitive, which is itself a P11-pivoted audit result, not a proof; and the identification carries no delivered prediction yet. Distinctive, structural, unconfirmed.*

**Unitarity is emergent, not fundamental (Report §6).** The arrow that forbids reversibility forbids erasure: every commitment is a permanent, un-erasable record. Reversible unitary quantum mechanics is the *between-commitments* description; the substrate itself is irreversible. This is why ED preserves black-hole information without a fundamental Hilbert-space unitarity (the record survives; no firewall). It matters here because the gravitational-collapse program also gives up exact unitarity — deviations from Schrödinger evolution are the whole point — and ED gives that up at the foundational level, by design.

Hold three things from this section: **the arrow selects definiteness (einselection); the arrow is gravity's time (the khronon); and these are one primitive.** They are what ED brings into the comparison.

## §3 — The three neighbors, one at a time

### 3.1 Diósi (1987): gravity as fundamental indeterminacy

Diósi's move is the one ED most cleanly shares. He *does not quantize* the gravitational field. Instead he posits that the Newtonian potential is a classical (c-number) field carrying a universal, fundamental indeterminacy — a "gravitational white noise," `⟨φ(r,t)φ(r',t')⟩ = ℏG|r−r'|⁻¹δ(t−t')`. Feeding that noise into the Schrödinger evolution of a mass distribution yields a master equation with a damping term that destroys interference between states of different mass distribution, on a characteristic time `τ_d ≈ ℏ/[U(x−x') − U(0)] = ℏ/E_G`. Atoms are untouched (`E_G` negligible); a colloidal grain loses coherence at its own size. (Diósi flags the point-mass divergence — `E_G → ∞` for point masses — the regularization problem that dogs the whole program.)

**Shared:** the refusal to quantize gravity, and a universal, mass-scaled, physical decoherence. **Divergence:** Diósi's collapse is a stochastic *field* injected as an addition to quantum mechanics — a noise term bolted onto the Schrödinger equation. ED's collapse is *internal to the ontology*: it is the arrow, commitment, the same primitive that constitutes time and selects the pointer basis. ED does not add a noise to QM; ED's QM is the emergent, between-commitments description of a substrate whose fundamental act is the commitment. Diósi supplies a mechanism ED would call a *shadow* — a coarse-grained effective description — of something more primitive.

### 3.2 Penrose (1996): the ill-defined time, and ED's answer to it

Penrose's is the deepest version, and it is the one that touches ED's nerve directly. Take a lump of material in a quantum superposition of two locations. Each location, on its own, is a stationary state with a well-defined mass distribution and therefore a well-defined spacetime geometry. The superposition is thus a superposition of **two different spacetimes**. Now ask for the state's energy, i.e. the generator of time-translation. In general relativity, by diffeomorphism invariance, there is no coordinate-independent way to identify the points of one spacetime with the points of the other — so there is **no well-defined time-translation operator for the superposed state.** That ill-definedness is an irreducible uncertainty in the state's energy, which in the Newtonian limit equals `E_Δ`, the gravitational self-energy of the *difference* between the two mass distributions. By the energy–time relation, the superposition is not stationary after all: it decays, like an unstable particle, in a lifetime

$$T \approx \frac{\hbar}{E_\Delta}.$$

Penrose is careful that this is not a theory of collapse, only an argument for the *level* at which gravity must force deviations from unitary quantum mechanics; and he argues (against Diósi) that his version should conserve energy, protected by general covariance.

Here is the ED point, and it is the sharpest paragraph in this note. **Penrose's mechanism is the ill-definedness of a preferred time.** His superposition collapses *because* there is no fact about which time is flowing across the two superposed spacetimes. Event Density's entire foundation is that there **is** such a fact: the arrow, the substrate's direction of commitment, is a primitive preferred time — and (Report §6) it is *the same* preferred time gravity's khronon foliation picks out. So ED does not stand beside Penrose as a rival; **ED supplies exactly the object whose absence drives his argument.** Read Penrose forward: "there is no well-defined time across the superposition, therefore it must reduce." Read ED: "there is a well-defined committed time — the arrow — and the commitment in that time *is* the reduction." ED locates the collapse in the **time** itself (the committed arrow), one step *upstream* of the **energy** `E_Δ` that Penrose derives as the time-ambiguity's consequence. Penrose finds the collapse in the energy uncertainty; ED finds it in the resolution of the very time-ambiguity Penrose starts from.

This is why the resonance is not decorative. The two accounts are about the *same variable* — the preferred time of a superposed gravitational configuration — approached from opposite directions: Penrose from its absence in GR, ED from its presence in the substrate.

### 3.3 Hossenfelder (2025): the closest ontology, a different price for locality

Hossenfelder's founding assumption reads like ED translated into Hilbert-space language: **matter and geometry are the same quantum state, and geometry carries no independent degrees of freedom** — the geometry is fully determined by the matter and vice versa (she reaches this through Wheeler's geons, Causal Fermion Systems, Shape Dynamics, Geometric Unity). This *is* ED's ontology: in ED geometry is not a separate field but the emergent shadow of the participation structure, `g ~ 1/b`; matter (participation) and geometry are two readings of one substrate. Of the three neighbors, hers is the closest match at the level of *what the world is made of*.

She then makes an argument ED can appreciate mechanically. Canonical quantum gravity *entangles* matter and geometry under unitary evolution — but her assumption demands they stay a *product* (the same information on both sides). The two are incompatible, so the state must **evolve off the Schrödinger equation**: it collapses, locally, by minimizing the residual (the deviation from Schrödinger evolution) subject to the product constraint. This is her collapse.

**What ED offers her:** a substrate. Her matter-equals-geometry constraint is an assumption she posits (and openly cannot derive); ED gives it a *reason* — geometry is `1/b`, literally what the participation density makes, so the "product constraint" is not imposed but structural. ED is a candidate for the underlying theory whose existence she assumes.

**Where they cleanly diverge, and this is the fork to own:** Hossenfelder confronts the deepest problem in the whole gravitational-collapse program — *non-locality*. As she puts it, once the gravitational self-energy is large enough to matter, "it is too late to collapse the state locally," because the superposition is already spread out and a local collapse would have to reach across space instantaneously. Her fix is **superdeterminism**: the collapse is governed by a *teleological* action that depends on the measurement's end state, which violates measurement independence and thereby (per Bell) permits a local model. ED does not pay this price. ED's collapse is a **local substrate event** — each commitment happens at a locus — and it is **forward-causal**: the arrow runs one way, no end-state input. ED's A1 result already establishes that the controlled information capacity between causally disconnected substrate regions is *exactly zero*, so locality is built in, not bought. And ED's outcomes are **stochastic at the moment of commitment**, not fixed by hidden variables. So ED threads Hossenfelder's needle — a local, physical collapse — with the *forward arrow* rather than with a superdeterministic, end-state-dependent hidden variable. This is a real philosophical fork: she is superdeterministic; ED is arrow-committed and genuinely stochastic. It should be stated plainly, as a difference of foundations, not smoothed over.

## §4 — The unifying difference: time, not energy; one primitive, not a causal chain

Step back from the three and the common structure of all of them appears. Diósi, Penrose, and Hossenfelder each say: **gravity → collapse.** There is quantum mechanics, and there is a gravitational effect *added* to it (a noise, an energy uncertainty, an off-Schrödinger residual) that causes the collapse, with `E_G` setting its scale. Collapse is a *consequence* of gravity.

Event Density says something structurally different: **the committed arrow-time is gravity's time and is the pointer-basis selector — one primitive, two hats, not a causal chain.** Definiteness and gravitational time are not cause and effect; they are the same thing seen in two sectors. This is why ED does not *add* a collapse term to the Schrödinger equation: in ED the Schrödinger equation is the emergent, between-commitments description, and the commitment (the collapse) is the more fundamental act, not a correction to it. And it is why ED dissolves the problem of time rather than reconciling it: there is one preferred time, it is the arrow, and it does every job that needs a preferred time — including making outcomes definite and running gravity's foliation.

The collapse *variable* is therefore different. For the program, it is **energy** (`E_G`, the gravitational self-energy of the difference). For ED, it is **time** (the committed arrow), from which any energy scale would be a derived consequence — which is exactly the direction Penrose's own argument runs (time-ambiguity *first*, energy uncertainty *derived*). ED's claim is that the program has been working one level downstream of the real variable.

## §5 — The honest gap: ED owes them a number

Everything above is ED's advantage in *depth of reason*. Here is the program's advantage in *sharpness of result*, and it is not small.

Diósi and Penrose both deliver the same quantitative, parameter-free, falsifiable prediction: `τ ≈ ℏ/E_G`. That is a number, testable in the laboratory. And it *is* being tested. A 2020 underground experiment searched for the spontaneous radiation the collapse must emit and found none at the predicted level, **excluding the simplest, parameter-free (natural-regularization) Diósi–Penrose model**. Hossenfelder's is likewise built to be "local, parameter-free, and testable." These models put a number on the table and let the world shoot at it. That is what a physical theory is supposed to do, and it is why the program is taken seriously.

Event Density, at present, does **not** deliver this number. ED's account of collapse is *qualitative*: the arrow selects definiteness, einselection is primitive, the pointer basis is the channel basis. What ED has *not* done is compute a **collapse rate** and check it against `ℏ/E_G`. The one quantitative decoherence result ED has produced — the thermal-decoherence probe of the dark-sector work — gave a rate proportional to **velocity dispersion** (P11-commitment-on-encounter), *not* proportional to `E_G`. So on the specific battlefield where Diósi, Penrose, and Hossenfelder fight, ED currently has a deeper story and a missing number.

This is the note's honest center, and it is also its opportunity. The work is well-posed:

1. **Derive ED's collapse rate.** For a mass in a superposition of two configurations, compute the commitment/einselection rate from the substrate — how fast the arrow individuates the superposed configuration into one channel. This is the ED analog of `τ`.
2. **Compare to `ℏ/E_G`.** Either ED *recovers* the Diósi–Penrose scaling — in which case ED gives that scaling a deeper origin (the arrow, not a noise or an energy uncertainty), and inherits its experimental status — or ED *predicts something different* — a rate set by the substrate's commitment structure (its emergent-metric response, or a velocity/temperature dependence like the thermal-decoherence probe) rather than by `E_G`. A robust divergence from `ℏ/E_G` would be a **distinctive, falsifiable ED prediction** in the exact sector where the simplest DP model is already in trouble.

Until that calculation exists, the honest statement is: ED reframes *why* gravitational state reduction happens more deeply than any of the three, and owes them the *rate* at which it happens. And the standing Report §6 caveat holds alongside it — the einselection-arrow = khronon-arrow identification is a structural synthesis, not forced by a deeper theorem, with no delivered prediction yet. Two honest debts, one deep reframe.

## §6 — Position

Event Density is a member of the gravitational-collapse program, not a bystander to it, and it enters agreeing on the two convictions that define the program: geometry is not an independent quantized field, and collapse is a real physical event rooted in gravity. Where it parts company is on the *shape* of the claim. Diósi, Penrose, and Hossenfelder say gravity causes collapse and put the scale in an energy, `E_G`. ED says the definiteness of outcomes and the flow of gravitational time are one primitive — the arrow of commitment, which is the khronon — so the collapse variable is time, and the "cause" is not a cause but an identity. Read against Penrose specifically, ED supplies the well-defined preferred time whose *absence* drives his argument, and so reads less as his competitor than as the resolution of the gap he opens. Read against Hossenfelder, ED shares her matter-equals-geometry ontology and gives it a substrate, while buying locality with a forward arrow instead of her superdeterminism. And read against all three, ED currently owes what they deliver: a collapse rate, and the falsifiable confrontation that comes with it.

Penrose and Hossenfelder are, in short, Event Density's *measurement-side* interlocutors, exactly as Berezhiani and Khoury are its *dark-matter-side* ones — the nearest published neighbors on each face of the same underlying bet, that gravity and the deep structure of the quantum world are one problem. Positioning ED honestly against them, deep reason and owed number alike, is how that bet gets taken seriously.

## §7 — Falsifiers and tiers

**Falsifiers of the ED position here:**
- **F1.** A computed ED collapse rate that neither recovers `ℏ/E_G` nor matches any observed decoherence — i.e. ED gets the *rate* wrong once it is derived. (Currently the rate is not derived; this is a pending, not a passed, test.)
- **F2.** A demonstration that the einselection arrow and the gravitational khronon *cannot* be the same object (e.g. the pointer basis and the gravitational foliation shown to require distinct, non-identifiable time structures) — collapses the unification move.
- **F3.** Experimental confirmation of the *parameter-free* Diósi–Penrose `ℏ/E_G` collapse with an energy-conservation signature that ED's forward-arrow, recording-based mechanism cannot accommodate.
- **F4.** A demonstration that a local, forward-causal, stochastic collapse is impossible (that Hossenfelder's superdeterminism, or an equivalent non-locality, is *forced*) — would remove ED's claimed advantage over her.

**Tiers:**
- The arrow selects the pointer basis (einselection primitive) — **reconstruction** (Gleason/Piron–Solèr; Solèr rigor residual). Report §4.
- The arrow is gravity's time; measurement-definiteness and gravitational-time are one primitive — **structural synthesis, unconfirmed**; not forced by a deeper theorem; no delivered prediction. Report §6.
- Geometry emergent (`g ~ 1/b`; matter-equals-geometry, shared with Hossenfelder) — **standing** (curvature-emergence, static half closed).
- Locality without superdeterminism (local commitment + A1 capacity-zero + stochastic-at-commitment) — **grounded** (A1 measured; the forward arrow is P11).
- A quantitative collapse rate `τ` (vs `ℏ/E_G`) — **not delivered** (the gap; the note's central owed work).
