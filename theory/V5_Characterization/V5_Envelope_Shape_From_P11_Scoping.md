## The angle

Standard mathematical physics already answers a closely related question: why do some fields decay exponentially with distance (Yukawa-type screening) while others decay as a power law (Coulomb/gravity-type, no screening)? The answer is well-established: **a field is the Green's function of some underlying operator, and an operator with a spectral gap (a nonzero minimum "mass" term) gives an exponentially-decaying Green's function; a gapless operator gives a power-law one.** This isn't an ED-specific claim — it's textbook (screened vs. unscreened potentials, massive vs. massless propagators).

ED already has a primitive that behaves like exactly the kind of ingredient that introduces a gap: **P11 (commitment irreversibility)** is a one-way, absorbing process — participation collapses to a single channel at commitment events, an irreversible "loss" from the pre-commitment state. A process with a built-in one-way loss/absorption term is precisely the kind of thing that turns a gapless (power-law) propagator into a gapped (exponential) one in every standard example of this pattern (damped wave equations, absorbing random walks, screened diffusion).

**The hypothesis, stated plainly: V1/V5's envelope decays exponentially (not as a power law) because P11's irreversibility acts as an absorption/gap term in whatever discrete equation the kernel is secretly the Green's function of — and if this can be made rigorous, the decay RATE (not just the shape) would be set by the commitment rate, not left as a free parameter.**

## What would need to be shown, honestly

2. **Show P11's commitment events enter that equation as an absorption/loss term** — not just assert it by analogy, but actually locate the term and show it has the right mathematical form (a decay/damping coefficient, not just "something happens sometimes").
3. **Solve for (or characterize) the resulting Green's function**, and check it's consistent with Theorem N1's already-established admissible-class properties (bounded, no delta-function, no infinite-width) — this is a necessary consistency check, not sufficient on its own.
4. **Identify what quantity in the equation plays the role of "the gap"** — is it a rate quantity already present elsewhere in the corpus (e.g., related to P04's bandwidth-decay-per-commitment), or would it require inheriting a new empirical number, same as every other regime-specific τ_V5 value already in the corpus?

## First-pass attempt (same session, 2026-07-06) — partial, structural, not yet closed

Worked through the actual mathematics rather than leaving this as a bare hypothesis.

**The standard result.** A field obeying a "screened" equation — schematically $(-\nabla^2 + m^2)G = \delta$, where $m^2 > 0$ is a gap/mass term — has a Green's function that decays exponentially, at rate set by $m$. In one spatial dimension specifically (exactly the geometry every probe tonight used), the Green's function of this equation is *exactly* $G(x) \propto e^{-m|x|}$ — pure exponential, no polynomial prefactor, no other structure. This is standard, textbook mathematics (the screened/Yukawa propagator), not an ED-specific claim.

**A real consistency check, not engineered.** The exponential form used, without this reasoning in hand, in tonight's `dwell_field_decay_probe.py` (`k_field · exp(-|x|/xi)`) is *exactly* this 1D screened-propagator shape. That wasn't picked because it happened to work — it's independently the correct functional form for a 1D gapped process. That's a genuine, non-trivial match, worth taking seriously.

**Where the gap ($m^2$) would come from.** P11 (commitment irreversibility) is the one ED primitive that behaves like a built-in loss/reset process — a chain's participation is irreversibly collapsed at commitment events. Reading $m^2$ as proportional to the commitment *rate* $\Gamma$ (roughly, how often a chain commits per unit substrate-time) gives a physically sensible identification: $\xi = 1/m \sim c/\Gamma$ — **the decay length is set by how far a chain's causal influence can travel before its next irreversible commitment resets it.** This is a genuinely ED-native story (reach limited by the arrow of time itself, not an arbitrary dial), not an imported analogy.

**What is NOT yet shown, stated plainly.** This is a *partial, structural* result, not a closure:
1. The underlying equation itself — that participation amplitude on the substrate graph obeys a discrete wave/diffusion-type equation with nearest-neighbor coupling in the first place — has not been derived from P02+P04+P07. It's a natural, common choice for something living on a locally-coupled graph, but "natural" is not "forced." This is the load-bearing gap.
2. P11's commitment events are discrete, stochastic, phase-randomizing events — not manifestly the same mathematical object as a smooth continuum absorption *rate* term in a differential equation. Showing the coarse-grained limit of many discrete commitment events genuinely reduces to a term of the right form ($m^2\psi$, not something else) is a real, separate step, not yet done.
3. Even if both of the above go through, this predicts *exponential shape*, not a numerical value for $\Gamma$ itself — the pattern would still be form-FORCED, value-INHERITED, same as everywhere else in ED. That is a fully acceptable, expected outcome (not a weakness) — it would still be real progress, turning an arbitrary shape-and-scale unknown into a shape-explained, scale-tied-to-a-named-physical-rate unknown.

## Second pass (same session, 2026-07-06) — building step 1 directly, and catching a real error in the first pass

**Error caught and fixed.** The first pass wrote $\xi \sim c/\Gamma$ by loose analogy without picking a specific transport equation. That was sloppy, and the actual answer depends on which of two standard transport types applies:

- **Diffusive transport + absorption** (a random walk that also dies at rate $\Gamma$): the correct, verified calculation (the absorption-weighted, time-integrated propagator — the resolvent of $-D\partial_x^2 + \Gamma$) gives $G(x) \propto e^{-|x|/\xi}$ with $\xi = \sqrt{D/\Gamma}$ — a **square-root** relationship, not linear.
- **Ballistic transport + absorption** (a front moving at fixed speed $c$ that dies at rate $\Gamma$ per unit time): survival probability to reach distance $x$ is $e^{-\Gamma t} = e^{-\Gamma x/c}$ (since $x=ct$ for ballistic motion) — exponential with $\xi = c/\Gamma$, **linear**. This was the first pass's (correct, but unjustified-at-the-time) guess.

**Which one applies is not a free choice — tonight's own probes already answered it.** Every dwell/field probe run tonight (`dwell_channel_mass_probe.py`, `dwell_field_decay_probe.py`, `dwell_ambient_density_probe.py`) confirmed the certified substrate's individual fronts move **ballistically** — one hop per step when not dwelling, not diffusively spreading. That's not an assumption for this argument to import; it's a directly observed, already-established fact about the actual running code. **So the ballistic-transport case is the empirically correct one for V1 (single-chain worldline reach): $\xi = c/\Gamma$, confirmed linear, not the diffusive square-root form.** (V5, being a genuinely cross-chain, possibly multi-path aggregate quantity, may be a separate question — flagged, not resolved here.)

**Attempting step 1 (the load-bearing gap from the first pass): building the transport equation from P02+P04+P07 directly, not just asserting it's natural.**

- P02 gives the primitive object: a chain's participation $(C,K,u,t)$ — an amplitude living at a locus, on a channel.
- P07 gives which loci are connected at all: channels are structurally distinct objects, and (per the graph-representation already used and tested tonight in the certified simulator) a channel connects two loci — this is the substrate-graph's edge structure, not an assumption invented for this argument; it's the same edge structure `evaluation/Bits/simulator/graph.py` already encodes.
- P04 gives the transport *strength*: bandwidth $b_K(u)$ is "the substrate-level scalar carrier of amount of participation" — reading the hopping weight between adjacent loci as proportional to the shared channel's bandwidth is not an arbitrary add-on, it's the most direct possible use of what P04 already says bandwidth *is for*.
- Putting these together gives, with no additional postulate beyond "amplitude moves along existing channels, weighted by how much participation-carrying-capacity (P04) each channel has": $\psi(v, t+dt) = \sum_{u \sim v} b_{K}(u,v)\,\psi(u,t)$ — a standard weighted-graph transport equation, built entirely from P02 (the amplitude object), P04 (the weights), and P07 (which pairs of loci are connected at all). **This is a real construction, not an assertion of naturalness** — every piece traces to a named primitive's own stated content.

**What's still open, honestly, after this pass:** this shows *a* transport equation follows fairly directly from P02+P04+P07 — it does not yet show this is the *unique* equation those primitives permit (a different weighting or coupling rule might also be consistent with the bare primitive text, and ruling those out hasn't been attempted). And step 2 from the first pass — showing many discrete P11 commitment events coarse-grain into a smooth absorption rate $\Gamma$ — connects directly to the corpus's own **already-established two-layer coarse-graining framework** ([[project_two_layer_coarsegraining]]: substrate → layer 1 kinetic/transport, non-Gaussian → layer 2 hydrodynamic/smooth). P11's commitment events are exactly layer-1 (discrete, one-way, non-Gaussian) content; a smooth rate $\Gamma$ is exactly a layer-2 (coarse-grained) description of them. This means the coarse-graining step isn't a new, invented move for this argument — it's an application of a mechanism the corpus already has a name and track record for, applied to a target (V1's envelope) nobody had pointed it at before. Still not *done* — the actual coarse-graining calculation (do many independent P11 events really average to a clean rate, or does something else happen, the way layer-1→layer-2 has been found non-trivial elsewhere in the corpus, e.g. the GRF-Gaussianity test's negative result) has not been run.

## Third pass (same session, 2026-07-06) — direct empirical test, and a real flaw found in the derivation

Attempted to test the whole argument directly on the certified substrate rather than resting on the math alone: perturb the background density at one locus by a small, fixed amount, run many independent fronts through the (otherwise identical) chain, and check whether the resulting difference from an unperturbed control run actually decays exponentially with distance from the perturbation, the way the argument predicts.

**Result: inconclusive, not confirming.** Neither an exponential fit nor a power-law fit came anywhere close to explaining the data (R² ≈ 0.09 and 0.03 respectively — both indistinguishable from noise). The measured difference doesn't show a clean decaying pattern with distance from the perturbation at all.

**Digging into why revealed a real flaw in the second-pass derivation, not just an untested assumption.** The transport equation built in the second pass — $\psi(v,t+dt) = \sum_u b(u,v)\psi(u,t)$ — implicitly assumes amplitude spreads smoothly and simultaneously to every neighbor, weighted by bandwidth, the way heat diffuses through a solid. **That is not what the certified update rule actually does.** The real dynamics (`apply_update` in `evaluation/Bits/simulator/update.py`) pick exactly *one* winning candidate per active front per step and commit fully to it — a hard, discrete, winner-take-all choice, not a smooth weighted spread across all neighbors at once. These are genuinely different mathematical objects: a linear diffusion equation versus a discrete argmax/competition process. **The screened-Green's-function machinery (which needs a genuine linear PDE/diffusion structure to produce its exponential solution) does not straightforwardly apply to a winner-take-all process**, and this is very plausibly why the direct empirical test found no clean signal — the equation the argument was built on doesn't actually govern this substrate's real behavior.

**A further, deeper complication surfaced in the same investigation:** the certified substrate has no built-in passive "spreading" mechanism for density at all. `rho` changes *only* at nodes that get directly committed to (`commit()` is "the SOLE rho writer") — a perturbation sitting at a locus no front ever visits simply stays exactly where it was placed, unchanged, forever. There is no diffusion-like process for it to decay through in the first place. This means the whole framing — "V1/V5 is the Green's function of some passively-propagating field" — may not match this substrate's actual character at all. Influence here propagates only through where active fronts actually go, not through a background field spreading on its own. V1/V5's kernel is fundamentally about a chain's own memory (V1) or cross-chain correlation (V5) — closer to a statement about *trajectories* than about a diffusing field — and a fair next attempt would need to start from that, not from a diffusion-equation analogy borrowed from continuum field theory.
