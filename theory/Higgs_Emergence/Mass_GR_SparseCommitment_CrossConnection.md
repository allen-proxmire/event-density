# Cross-connection: is the mass-memory fade rate the same quantity as the GR sparse-commitment parameter?

**Date:** 2026-07-06 (AP-directed "#1" investigation)
**Status:** Verdict (b) — plausibly the same, corroborated by the corpus, NOT proven. AND a new, specific dependency-check gap found in the process.

## The hypothesis (AP, from tonight's seventh-pass bank)

For a freely propagating chain, its mass-like sluggishness (intrinsic memory fade rate) would be set by the *sparse* commitment rate. ED's Phase-3 GR α₁/α₂ preferred-frame-safety argument *also* hinges on a sparse-commitment parameter. Hypothesis: **these are two faces of one underived quantity** — pin either, pin both, tying particle mass to gravitational safety through one number.

## What is genuinely shared, and solid (traceable in the corpus)

Both quantities are built from the **same P04 "commitment-reserve band"** (`b_res`, the bandwidth budget drawn down one-way by P11 commitment events), and both carry the **same dimensionless factor `k₁₁`** (the commitment-pinning efficiency) used consistently across the corpus:

- GR khronon weight: `λ = c₂ = f²/M_P² = (k₁₁/s₀₂)·ρ_event/ρ_Planck` (`Phase3_GR_LambdaOfRho_Derivation.md`), where `f²` (density-dependent, sparse-P11) comes from `b_res`, `M_P²` (density-independent, always-on) from P02 adjacency, and `ρ_event` = local density of P11 commitment events.
- Near-matter commitment rate: `κ = k₁₁·c/ℓ_P` (`Phase3_GR_PinningKappaD.md`) — **the same `k₁₁`**.
- Primitive commitment rate: `Γ_commit ~ b_int/(commitment-reserve budget)` (`primitives/concepts/commitment.md`) — divides by **the same `b_res` band**.

So `λ` = (band prefactor) × (sparse `Γ_commit`)/(Planck rate). The mass fade rate and the GR `λ` both reduce to "sparse commitment rate ÷ substrate-fast rate," through the same band and the same `k₁₁`. **The strongest true form of the hypothesis:** `k₁₁` is a single dimensionless unknown that, if derived once, cascades across particle mass, GR α₁ screening, QC decoherence, and QFT vertex rates simultaneously. That is real and traceable.

## What is NOT shared — the honest ceiling on "pin one, pin both"

The two quantities do **not** collapse to a single scalar even if `k₁₁` is shared, because each carries a *separate* underived magnitude:

- Mass fade rate uses the vacuum-sparse `ρ_event` along a chain's own worldline.
- GR `α₁` uses the Solar-System vacuum `ρ_event^vac` (the one explicitly-open EFT/off-lattice magnitude).

Those are different physical densities in different regimes, both currently unpinned. **"Pin one, pin both" is true only for the `k₁₁` piece, not for the magnitudes.** The connection was also confirmed genuinely novel — no corpus file before tonight ever linked commitment sparseness to particle mass.

**Verdict: (b) plausibly the same, specifically the same band structure + same `k₁₁` + same `ρ_event/ρ_Planck` scaling, but two regime-specializations, not one proven scalar.** Promoting to (a) requires showing the *same numerical* `k₁₁` governs both (Parked_Routes item 5).

## The new catch found while checking this — a real, specific dependency gap

The shared link runs through the P04 **commitment-reserve band**. That four-band P04 structure was flagged three separate times tonight as **non-canonical/archived** (canonical P04 is a bare additive scalar). `FourBand_Dependency_Check.md` (2026-07-05) cleared the *mass* sector: there the bands are a decorative summation index, collapse them and σ_τ / M1 / M2 / Paper_113 all survive on canonical single-scalar P04.

**But that dependency check never covered the Phase-3 GR band-partition work — and that is exactly where the bands are load-bearing, not decorative.** The GR α₁ mechanism *is* the split between the always-on adjacency band (`M_P²`) and the sparse commitment-reserve band (`f²`); the measured `c₂ ∝ ρ_event/ρ_Planck` scaling and the whole α₁ safety verdict depend on distinguishing those two bands. Unlike the mass sector, you cannot collapse them to one scalar and keep the result — the density-independent-vs-density-dependent contrast is the mechanism.

**The specific open question (newly identified, not yet checked):** is the GR two-band split (always-on adjacency vs sparse commitment-reserve) the *archived four-band structure* (a problem — it would inherit the non-canonical primitive-forcing claim), or is it re-expressible as a **canonical P02-vs-P11 decomposition** (fine — P02 adjacency-sharing and P11 commitment events are genuinely distinct canonical primitives, so "bandwidth used for always-on P02 sharing" vs "bandwidth drawn by P11 commitments" is a legitimate split along primitive lines, not an archived-band claim)? This determines whether the GR α₁ safety result — and therefore the solid end of this cross-connection — rests on canonical primitives or on the retired M-series structure. **It is very plausibly fine (the P02-vs-P11 reading is natural), but it has not been verified, and the existing dependency check explicitly did not cover it.**

## Bottom line

Your cross-connection instinct is corroborated: mass and gravity really do share the same commitment-reserve band, the same `k₁₁`, and the same sparsity scaling — a genuinely novel, structurally-solid link. But (1) it's plausible-not-proven (the magnitudes stay separately unpinned; only `k₁₁` is truly shared), and (2) checking it surfaced that the GR side's band dependency was never cleared the way the mass side's was — a specific, cheap, high-value next check. The multi-simulator gap (target A9) still blocks a single-sim demonstration; this stays a paper-level identification.
